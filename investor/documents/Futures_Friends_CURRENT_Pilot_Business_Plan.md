# Futures Friends — current 38-child pilot business and funding plan

Prepared October 13, 2026 (model pilot-38-v5.1; staffing, tuition and the food, cleaning, utilities, tax-preparation and curriculum budgets owner-confirmed). One owned Independence, Missouri location, ages 2–5, all-day care for 38 children, twelve months of operations and a first-month checkpoint. This replaces the expanded $3,139,152.61 case and the discarded $700,000 refinance assumption. National rollout belongs to Phase 2.

## What the pilot proves
Futures Friends connects Booker, Lumi, Zuri and Bop to early literacy, movement, nutrition, belonging and family routines. Reuse the existing website, CRM, ATS, store, characters and learning materials. Complete parent/child access, curriculum records, approved character reminders and the operating controls needed for one center. Measure safe delivery, parent use, retention and actual cash margin before expanding.

## Revenue, year two and repayment
The owner confirmed on October 13, 2026 a locked-in rate of **$210/week per child**, converted at 52/12 to **$910/month** (52 billed weeks; no tuition increases assumed). Sibling discounts would reduce realized receipts. At 38 children and 96% cash collection, annual receipts are $398,361.60. First-year enrollment ramps 30, 34, 36, then 38 from month four; this is illustrative, not contracted demand.

Ongoing annual expenses remain $286,007.37 in year two before inflation and building payments. Operating cash is **$112,354.23** before mortgage, investor debt and profit tax. At 38 occupied places, break-even realized tuition is **$651.16/child/month**, before building/debt, with food held at the owner's $2,000.00/month cap and processing fees recalculated as enrollment changes. Many costs are one-time: $142,554.00 of launch and completion spending falls in months 1–5, against $286,007.37/year of recurring cost. Net cash turns positive in month 5, and cumulative pilot cash turns positive in month 17 at the month-12 rate, before building payments and debt. A positive repayment plan requires more than reaching this operating break-even. At the same costs, 38 places are the modeled ceiling, not an invitation to enroll beyond licensed capacity.

Monthly debt capacity = max(0, annual receipts − ongoing expenses − annual building payments) / 1.25 / 12. The 1.25 coverage hurdle is a planning screen, not a lender commitment. When the building payment is unknown, affordable loan capacity is unknown. The digital calculator shows loan payments and whether cash covers them; it does not deduct loan payments from gross revenue and call that profit. No permanent equity is offered. Rate, term, amortization, collateral and guarantees require an actual lender offer. A long term reduces payments but increases total interest; a balloon does not solve repayment without a documented exit.

The positive operating margin rests on planning assumptions that still need verification before any loan is sized: signed enrollment at the locked-in rate, actual bills, payroll and insurance, the building payment and lender terms. Existing tuition revenue must cover recurring operations after the first funded year. Unsigned merchandise, church/daycare licensing, grants or national expansion are excluded from debt underwriting. They remain Phase 2 opportunities, evaluated on their own unit economics.
