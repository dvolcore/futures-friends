## 1. The proposition

Futures Friends is a character-led early-learning program for children ages **2–5**, built for a pilot at one location and designed to extend into other daycare, church, home-daycare, and family settings. The brand joins story, classroom practice, movement, food education, family connection, merchandise, and a role-based digital platform.

The promise is simple: **Learn. Move. Explore. Belong.** A short story starts a week of play, conversation, observation, movement, nourishment, and take-home connection. Adults remain responsible for the relationship and the safety of the child. Technology supports the adult-mediated experience; it does not replace care or open-endedly converse with children.

### Tone and child respect

Stories are warm, funny, specific, and physically readable. A problem is small enough to repair: a tower wobbles, a turn feels hard, a new friend needs room, or a question needs testing. The first attempt may fail safely. Adults ask, wait, and protect the conditions for children to solve. No child is a mascot, diagnosis, behavior label, or sales device. Physical comedy is warned, soft, and supervised.

Repeatable cues include “___ coming through,” “Wobble Check… FLUMP,” and “Turtle tempo… go-go.” The current finite character-message library is adult-mediated and approved-message based. It is not an open-ended child chatbot.

## 3. Story engine and format

Every episode answers: What ordinary problem appears? Who owns the attempt? How does that strength overshoot? What does the friend feel or risk? Which clue opens a possibility? What changed action repairs the moment?

The episode rhythm is: cold open; welcome; visible problem; first attempt; wobble; pause for the child; second attempt; song or movement; repair; closing circle; book close; short tag. The working development target is approximately six minutes, with no more than three locations. The locked runtime belongs to the approved timed cut.

### Episode One: Booker Makes Room

Pip arrives for her first day with Mom and watches before entering. Booker tries to solve her feeling too quickly and overwhelms her. Ms. June helps him ask what Pip needs. He offers a small duck game and waits. When Pip misses Mom, he offers company instead of distraction. Pip retains agency and later asks Booker to save her place. The screenplay, table read, blocking, timing, approved assets, voice, and connected render remain acceptance gates.

## 4. Curriculum architecture

The learning loop is **Watch, Talk, Do, Play, Reinforce, Take Home**. Futures Friends connects literacy and language, mathematics, science, social-emotional learning, movement and health, nutrition, and creative arts. Five learning zones translate the story into a physical room:

- **Booker’s Reading Area:** books, sequence cards, vocabulary, marks, listening.
- **Lumi’s Calm Corner:** greeting, feeling language, boundaries, space/company choices.
- **Zuri’s Discovery Zone:** observation, prediction, comparison, nature, food inquiry.
- **Bop’s Movement Zone:** movement, construction, carrying, practical independence.
- **Eat the Rainbow:** family-style food language, color, choice, safe participation.

Developmental bands are practical rather than diagnostic. Ages 2–3 use objects, pointing, copying, and one anchor word. Ages 3–4 add simple predictions, counting to five, and feeling language. Ages 4–5 add cause and effect, retelling, comparison, and helper roles. “Not seen this week” is an observation entry, never a deficit label.

The source library describes 12 monthly units and 48 theme weeks, 36 recipe concepts, 27 family activities, and 17 printables. Unit and printable approvals are not yet recorded; Spanish printables remain draft or review status. These counts are library inventory, not completed or accredited delivery.

## 7. Meals, movement, and safety

Eat the Rainbow is an educational food framework, not medical advice, CACFP certification, or a substitute for a qualified food lead. A recipe release needs yield, portion, ingredients, allergen flags, choking-safe preparation, substitutions, temperature/holding requirements, crediting evidence, source/date, and reviewer. Adults handle knives, heat, raw proteins, and chemical agents. No child is pressured to taste.

Movement includes Turtle Tempo, Sun Stretch, Heart Hands, stop/go contrasts, outdoor play, carrying, balance, rhythm, cleanup, and seated or adaptive alternatives. The program is inclusive of children using mobility, visual, hearing, or AAC supports. Never force contact, breathing, balance, or a stretch.
