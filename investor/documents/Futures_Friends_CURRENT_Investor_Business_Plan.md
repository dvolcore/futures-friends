## 1. Executive case

Futures Friends is a bilingual early-learning system for children ages 2–5. It joins a character-led curriculum, family activities, movement, nutrition, classroom learning zones, physical merchandise, professional support, and a planned digital platform. Booker learns, Lumi belongs, Zuri explores, and Bop moves. The purpose is practical: give adults a usable rhythm for the day, give children a warm and memorable way into learning, and give families a clear connection to what happens in the room.

The first operating case is one pilot location. The reusable offer is intended to support future daycares, home daycares, churches, pre-K partners, families, and employers after the pilot proves safety, delivery, retention, unit economics, and demand. Those future channels are opportunities, not contracted receipts.

The proposed financing is a **$1,500,000 no-equity loan concept**. Principal, rate, points, draw schedule, collateral, covenants, repayment dates, and any revenue participation remain subject to an agreed instrument and counsel review. No permanent ownership is offered in this concept. No return is guaranteed.

The current full-scope model reconciles to **$3,139,152.61 gross first-year cash requirement** in the base case. The difference versus the proposed loan is **$1,639,152.61 before receipts, scope changes, and financing costs**. This is a scope and staging problem to solve before closing, not a gap to hide in a headline.

## 2. The customer problem

A childcare operator must keep children safe, meet ratios and rules, support different ages, communicate with families, train staff, and maintain a consistent program while the room is busy. A family wants to know what a child is learning and how to continue it at home. A church or home provider needs a right-sized program that looks professional without taking on a center-sized system.

Most tools solve only one part of that problem: administration, billing, a curriculum library, a classroom decoration package, or a family app. Futures Friends is designed as a connected operating and learning system. The claim is not that the product has already displaced those tools. The claim is that a coordinated character world, room experience, learning plan, adult support, and digital record can be tested as one offer.

## 3. Product and brand system

### 3.1 The four pillars

- **Booker — Learn:** early literacy, storytelling, listening, courage to try again, and language-rich routines.
- **Lumi — Belong:** social-emotional learning, empathy, calm, relationships, and participation.
- **Zuri — Explore:** curiosity, questions, nature, discovery, and hands-on investigation.
- **Bop — Move:** physical activity, self-regulation, healthy routines, and movement breaks.

The characters are a teaching language, not an open-ended chatbot. Child-facing communication uses a finite, adult-approved message library. Adults decide what appears, when it appears, and what action follows. No unrestricted child AI is part of the operating promise.

### 3.2 The learning day

The program connects a short story or character prompt to teacher talk, a hands-on activity, movement, an exploration zone, and a family take-home question. The same idea can appear in a classroom, a home daycare, a church program, or a family living room at a different level of complexity.

Core content areas include early literacy, social-emotional learning, movement, food and nutrition, art, music, nature, practical routines, and family engagement. Eat the Rainbow and movement plans are program components. The educator and registered-nurse reviews currently documented are limited to the materials reviewed; they are not accreditation, certification, medical advice, or regulatory approval.

### 3.3 Bilingual requirement

English and Spanish are product-wide requirements across parent materials, learning content, support, portals, key operations, labels, and training. Translations need human review before release. A translated draft is not an approved production asset, and Spanish availability must be tracked by asset and version.

### 3.4 Physical learning zones and merchandise

The room system translates the four pillars into coordinated carpets, posters, symbols, signs, books, plush, apparel, welcome materials, QR habit cards, and classroom tools. Branded carpets and matching posters are a potential B2B product for other organizations, subject to artwork approval, minimum quantities, deposits, production lead times, freight, storage, fulfillment, defects, and returns.

Catalog pricing is not margin. Supplier quotes, freight, payment fees, inventory terms, fulfillment labor, taxes, returns, and minimum order quantities must be captured before a product is sold as a profitable line. The current store direction is order-request based; a settled payment and fulfillment path remains a release gate.

## 4. Platform and operating architecture

The public website demonstrates brand, information architecture, activities, character world, family content, pilot-center information, commerce direction, portal concepts, and learning-zone language. It is visual/static evidence. It is not evidence that a hosted multi-tenant Hub, production enrollment system, billing flow, or live school platform is operating.

The planned platform roles are:

- **Director:** center setup, staffing visibility, program configuration, approvals, reports, and billing administration.
- **Teacher:** daily plans, assigned activities, observations, child work, classroom routines, and family notes within assigned permissions.
- **Parent or guardian:** consent, child link, family activities, messages, progress summaries, and account controls.
- **Adult-mediated Kid Mode:** approved activities, work submission, character prompts, and age-appropriate navigation; no unrestricted chat.
- **Support:** documented issue handling with limited access to the minimum information required.
- **Partner or HQ:** implementation, training, catalog, and aggregate service reporting without unauthorized tenant financial access.

A production release must prove authentication, role separation, organization isolation, persistence, billing, backups and restores, audit logs, accessibility, performance, security, privacy, consent, and real-device behavior. Parent and staff experiences must remain separate. A browser screen or local preview does not prove these controls.

### 4.1 CRM and ATS scope

The commercial system needs a CRM pipeline for center, church, home-provider, school, employer, and family leads; source and campaign tracking; contacts and organizations; discovery notes; demos; quotes; deposits; contracts; onboarding; support history; renewals; and revenue attribution. It needs role permissions, audit history, deduplication, import/export, email or messaging integration, consent records, and reporting.

The recruiting system needs positions, applicants, source tracking, structured screening, interview stages, notes, background-check status, credential and training records, references, offer records, onboarding tasks, reminders, and retention reporting. It must separate sensitive candidate information from child and family data. Build-versus-buy must be evaluated with published subscription prices, implementation costs, data ownership, export terms, and vendor security documentation.

## 5. Center workflow and service delivery

The first pilot should be treated as a measured implementation, not as proof of a scaled network. The operating sequence is:

1. Confirm the legal borrower, host agreement, license or exemption position, capacity, insurance, age mix, hours, and ratio plan.
2. Complete room and zone setup, approved materials, staff orientation, safeguarding procedures, and parent onboarding.
3. Run the daily plan for four weeks while recording attendance, completed activities, family participation, support requests, incidents, and staff time.
4. Review the evidence with the director, educators, families, curriculum reviewer, and operations owner.
5. Correct content, translations, workflows, or room materials before offering the package to another organization.

The package should be sold around a measurable outcome: a daily rhythm staff can follow, a parent can understand, and a director can inspect. Every setup quote should specify materials, training hours, implementation support, delivery dates, renewal scope, cancellation terms, and what the buyer must provide.

## 7. Revenue architecture

Revenue opportunities are staged and currently uncontracted:

1. pilot-center tuition and program operations;
2. implementation and recurring support for daycares and church programs;
3. recurring platform, family, and partner subscriptions after hosted role flows are accepted;
4. curriculum, training, and professional-development packages;
5. learning-zone kits, branded carpets, posters, classroom materials, QR habit packs, books, plush, apparel, and welcome materials;
6. tablet sales or rentals with device management, subject to device, privacy, safety, and legal review;
7. licensing and distribution of approved stories, characters, curriculum, and media;
8. future church, daycare, home-provider, school, and employer partnerships.

A center's tuition is operating revenue. A provider setup fee and monthly membership are service revenue. Merchandise is sales revenue. Licensing is contract revenue. These streams need separate ledgers, margins, fulfillment rules, refund policies, and customer support measurements.

No partner receipts, customer contracts, signed purchase orders, or recurring revenue should be presented as secured without documents. The forecast receipts are illustrative stress inputs, not actual sales. Recurring income is not passive: it requires onboarding, curriculum maintenance, support, billing, data protection, training, replacement materials, and a cancellation policy.

## 8. Go-to-market and proof plan

Start with the Independence and Kansas City area and a supported pilot. The first commercial proof should be one center or home provider with a defined room or zone, staff onboarding, parent orientation, four weeks of use, and a before-and-after report. A church-specific offer and a home-provider offer should have their own landing pages, package scope, and buyer language.

The sales sequence is: short demonstration; site and capacity discovery; written fit and quote; deposit; setup; staff training; parent launch; 30-day review; monthly renewal. The system should record inquiry source, tour attendance, deposit date, first payment, churn, support hours, and gross margin by offer.

Useful proof is signed pilot agreements, paid setup invoices, renewal rate, staff completion, parent participation, attendance, completed activities, incident and complaint trend, time saved by the director, response time, and contribution margin. Until those records exist, they are targets.

## 10. Debt stress and repayment discipline

The illustrative debt schedule models **$1,500,000 at 12% annual interest**, 12 months interest-only at **$15,000 per month**, then 60 months at approximately **$33,366.67 per month**. This is a model, not an offer or promise. It assumes full funding at closing; staged advances require a fresh schedule. No points, fees, capitalized interest, penalty, collateral, personal guaranty, or revenue participation should be implied until written.

The base scenario uses year-one illustrative net receipts of **$370,784.80** and year-one operating cash before debt of **-$874,057.38**. Minimum ending available cash is **-$7,925,030.01**; downside is **-$9,067,210.80** and upside is **-$6,323,896.04**. The zero-receipt case has a $10,659,950.60 cash shortfall beyond the modeled $1.5M opening loan draw. These shortfalls are additional capital needs, not total opening capital. All current stress cases fail repayment coverage. The responsible conclusion is to stage draws, reduce scope, secure verified receipts, or identify additional capital before representing repayment as viable.

The investor should see this weakness because it defines the decision: fund a gated proof phase with a revised draw and cash plan, or do not close the full-scope facility until the model is underwritten. No return is guaranteed.

## 11. Use of funds and staging logic

The $1.5M concept should be allocated only after the gross model is reconciled into approved phases. A practical sequence is:

- **Readiness:** legal entity, license/capacity evidence, host agreement, insurance, privacy and safeguarding review, vendor quotes, bank records, and a 13-week cash forecast.
- **Pilot completion:** one location, ages 2–5, staff preparation, approved content, room materials, parent onboarding, and real-device acceptance.
- **Platform acceptance:** hosted authentication, roles, organization isolation, persistence, billing, backups/restores, logs, accessibility, performance, security, privacy, and support workflows.
- **Content release:** scripts, character continuity, animation test, voice/music rights, captions, Spanish review, child-development review, and delivery archive.
- **Measured expansion:** only after attendance, retention, completed work, family participation, support load, contribution margin, and signed demand are documented.

The budget is not complete until every row is classified as committed, quoted, allowance, benchmark, or decision pending; duplicated scope is removed; taxes, freight, payment fees, and support labor are assigned; and the phase total fits the approved draw.

## 12. Competitive position

Brightwheel, Procare, and Lillio are established alternatives in center administration, billing, family communication, and learning support. Futures Friends should not win by pretending those products are incomplete. The test is whether an operator wants this particular connected learning program and the support to use it in the room.

| Buyer question | Established alternatives | Futures Friends case to prove |
|---|---|---|
| Can the operator manage attendance, billing, and family messages? | Existing administration platforms have mature workflows. | Integrate with or replace only a defined workflow after acceptance testing. |
| Can staff use a coherent classroom program? | Operators may already own curriculum and room materials. | Connect characters, zones, stories, food, movement, home activities, and training. |
| Can families see value beyond childcare? | Communication tools may show updates without a learning system. | Show a consistent classroom-to-home rhythm and measured participation. |
| Can a church or home provider use it at the right size? | Large systems may be too broad or too costly for a small program. | Offer a defined smaller implementation with clear support and renewal terms. |

The potential advantage is the combination of owned characters and curriculum, bilingual delivery, physical/digital integration, adult-mediated child safety, and provider support. It is potential until adoption, retention, outcomes, contracts, rights, and operating records prove it.

## 13. Risks and controls

**Capital mismatch:** The loan concept is smaller than the gross model. Control with phase gates, revised scope, additional capital analysis, and no undisclosed assumptions.

**Unsecured revenue:** Future partners, subscriptions, church programs, merchandise, licensing, and expansion are not receipts until contracted and collected. Control with signed pilots, deposits, invoices, and separate revenue ledgers.

**Operating record gap:** Capacity, enrollment, staffing, rent, food, and collections require primary records. Control with license, lease, roster, payroll, bank, insurance, and permit diligence.

**Platform readiness:** Public pages do not prove hosted multi-tenant operation. Control with role-flow tests, persistence tests, isolation tests, backups/restores, security review, accessibility, performance, and real-device sign-off.

**Child privacy and safeguarding:** Child data, photos, family accounts, tablet identifiers, and messages require counsel-reviewed consent, retention, access control, incident response, and vendor agreements. Control with a written data map and release gate.

**Content rights and cost:** Character, voice, music, animation, translation, and generation costs may overlap. Control with a rights chain, asset register, dated vendor scope, actual account debit reconciliation, and a non-additive envelope review.

**Staffing and professional claims:** Educator and nurse reviews do not create accreditation. Control with named reviewers, credential records, approval dates, and a clear boundary between advice, curriculum review, and regulatory approval.

## 14. Investor diligence and milestones

Before any draw, provide the legal borrower and IP records, license or exemption position, capacity and host agreement, insurance, current roster and attendance, tuition collection report, payroll and tax obligations, bank statements, debt schedule, vendor quotes, rights chain, data/privacy plan, and 13-week cash forecast.

The first 90-day milestone package should include a signed pilot scope, approved materials and translations, staff training record, parent consent flow, real-device test record, platform acceptance report, backup/restore test, first paid receipt, support log, and weekly cash reconciliation.

The first expansion decision should require a documented pilot review showing retention, attendance, completed activities, family engagement, support hours, unit economics, complaint and incident handling, and signed demand from the next organization. No automatic expansion should be funded from a slide assumption.

12% fixed annual interest is a negotiation starting point, not a lender quote or market-average claim. At the proposed rate, amortizing payments need $41,708.34 in monthly cash before debt for 1.25x coverage. All four current forecasts fail coverage.
