What actually pays the loan?

The recommended model combines center tuition and recurring subscriptions from other daycares and churches. The month-24 growth test models $7,014.34 in monthly cash before $4,982.01 debt service, or 1.41× coverage. At 40% partner contribution, the same account count produces 1.24×. Setup fees and merchandise are excluded from this bridge. Those figures are planning assumptions, not verified collections.

What has to be confirmed before funding?

Licensed capacity, enrollment and collection evidence, paid partner contracts, vendor quotes, staffing and support ownership, owner compensation, taxes and year-two spending. Portal privacy, records, backups and role behavior need production acceptance.

Why would a partner keep paying?

The proposed renewal value is useful monthly content, educator support, family communication and a clear service review. A bounded pilot should test usage, staff workload, family response and willingness to renew before scaling acquisition.

What is the advantage—and the weakness?

The opportunity is a connected character, learning, family and service experience under one brand. The weakness is execution evidence: strong artwork and broad scope do not establish paid demand, efficient support or recurring margins. Position the platform against larger providers through a measured pilot, rather than an unsupported superiority claim.

What does the lender receive?

The scenarios illustrate principal and interest over a defined 72-month schedule, with no equity or permanent ownership. Any higher-tier, finite performance-linked bonus would require negotiated terms and separate underwriting. The $500K case needs a distinct expansion and cash plan; it is not supported by the recommended rollout.
