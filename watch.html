Futures Friends episodes

The planned Season 1 micro-series: 3 to 6 minute episodes made to be watched together, followed by a question and a hands-on activity. No episode is finished yet: the classroom player below has the friends’ welcome video, and the Watch together shelf has more short videos and Bop’s movement breaks. Scheduled program minutes are at most 24 a week, under a 30-minute weekly ceiling; children 2 and younger do not watch.

Talk about it

A card for every episode

Three questions (before, during and after), one feeling word and one thing to try at home, for each Season One episode. Print one, or print the whole set.

Why talk about it? In a study of Daniel Tiger’s Neighborhood, children’s empathy grew only when their parents regularly talked with them about the show (Texas Tech University: Daniel Tiger and empathy). The American Academy of Pediatrics also puts watching together and choosing quality first (AAP (HealthyChildren.org): helping kids thrive in a digital world). Sources checked 5 October 2026.

SCREEN TIME, DEFINED

Scheduled minutes are not a limit

The program schedules fewer minutes than its ceiling. Each number below means one thing.

Scheduled program minutes
24 minutes a weekAt most one episode a day, Monday to Thursday, each up to 6 minutes, watched with a teacher. Friday has no episode. 4 x 6 = 24.
Weekly program ceiling
30 minutes a weekA Futures Friends standard. The 24 scheduled minutes sit under it; it is never a target.
In-care all-screens ceiling
30 minutes a weekOur all-screens cap for children 3 to 5 in care: not more than 30 minutes a week in total, only for learning or movement, never at meals or snacks, with an adult. Program minutes count against it, so other screen use in care has at most 6 minutes a week left.
Children through age 2
0 minutesNo screens in care for children 2 and younger: songs, puppets and cards instead.

These are Futures Friends standards; neither Kansas nor Missouri sets a screen-time limit, and a center's own stricter policy wins. Outside guidance, checked 2026-10-05: Caring for Our Children; CDC early care and education obesity-prevention standards; WHO guidelines on physical activity.
